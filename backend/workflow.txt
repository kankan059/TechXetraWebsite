System Overview and Operating Principles
The entire backend acts as the central, authoritative brain of the system. The frontend website is never trusted with critical business logic; whether an event has open seats, what an item costs, whether a user has permission to perform an action, or whether a ticket is authentic, the backend makes every decision. All database updates execute in atomic batches, meaning multi-step operations either complete 100% or cancel cleanly without leaving partial records or corrupted data. Every sensitive action—including payment approvals, ticket corrections, check-ins, and score updates—is permanently recorded in tamper-proof, append-only audit logs that cannot be modified or deleted.

User Onboarding and Profile Setup
The flow begins when a participant signs in securely using Google OAuth. The backend exchanges their Google credentials for a protected, HTTP-only session cookie so the user stays logged in without exposing raw tokens in the browser. Before buying passes or registering for events, the user completes their profile with their full name, phone number, and student status. To prevent document fraud and keep operations simple, the system does not accept uploaded ID files. Instead, participants claiming student pricing agree to bring their physical college ID card to the venue on the day of the fest. Once a user purchases a student-tier pass or has their student status confirmed, their student profile fields are permanently locked from further editing to prevent tampering.

Pass Types and Campus Entry Rules
The system manages two distinct types of passes. A Gate Pass provides general admission to the campus across all three days of the festival with re-entry privileges; attendees can enter and leave multiple times, and each entry is recorded in the logs. An Event Pass admits the holder to a specific registered event venue and automatically grants campus entry on that event's day alone, meaning attendees do not need to buy a separate gate pass for days they have an event. Participants who want campus access on days when they have no scheduled events can purchase an additional gate pass.

Event Registrations, Teams, and Capacity Control
The catalog supports events across multiple categories, including Technical, Cultural, Sports, E-Sports, Workshops, and Management. Each event has configured price tiers (Free, Per-Participant, or Per-Team) and a strict capacity limit to prevent overcrowding. For individual events, participants register directly. For team events, a team leader creates a draft team and invites members by email. Teammates log into their own dashboards to accept or decline the invitation. Once the team reaches the required size and members are confirmed, the leader locks the roster and proceeds to checkout. The backend strictly prevents participants from joining or leading multiple active teams for the same event.

Checkout and the 15-Minute Seat Hold
When a buyer proceeds to checkout, the backend calculates the exact total in integer paise to eliminate rounding mistakes. At this moment, the system initiates an atomic fifteen-minute reservation, temporarily holding the selected event seats or passes under an unpaid status. This countdown guarantees the buyer a dedicated window to complete their payment without losing their spot to someone else. If the buyer abandons the purchase or fails to submit payment details before the timer runs out, the held seats are released back into the open pool.

The Payment Workflow: Zero-Fee Manual UPI
To avoid third-party payment gateway transaction fees (typically 2% to 3%) and avoid commercial business registration delays, the system currently runs a direct-to-bank manual UPI payment flow. During checkout, the website displays the official UPI ID, a dynamic payment QR code, and a mobile deep link. The buyer opens their preferred UPI app (such as Google Pay, PhonePe, or Paytm), transfers the exact amount directly to the bank account, and returns to the website to submit their twelve-digit UPI transaction reference number (UTR). The backend accepts this reference, marks the purchase as pending verification, and enforces a strict database uniqueness check so the same transaction number can never be submitted twice. Submitting a transaction ID does not immediately grant admission; it places the order into the verification queue.

Staff Verification and Guaranteed Ticket Fulfillment
Authorized staff members monitor a dedicated payment review dashboard to verify incoming submissions against official bank statements. Reviewers confirm that the money actually arrived, the amount matches the order, and the transaction ID is genuine. Staff members are strictly barred from reviewing or approving their own purchases. If a transaction is invalid, the reviewer rejects it with an explicit reason, and the buyer is alerted on their dashboard to correct the transaction ID or contact support. When a reviewer approves a payment, the purchase transitions to paid, and the backend immediately generates digital tickets equipped with unique, cryptographically signed (HMAC-SHA256) QR tokens. If ticket generation encounters an unexpected system error, the purchase remains safely recorded as paid. The system guarantees that payment truth is durable and never downgraded to unpaid, allowing staff to safely retry ticket fulfillment without asking the buyer to pay again.

Alternative Option: Automated Payment Gateway (Razorpay)
If management prefers a fully automated checkout instead of manual staff verification, the architecture can incorporate a payment gateway like Razorpay. Under this model, the backend creates an order through Razorpay's API, the user pays via a hosted checkout popup, and the backend verifies the digital payment signature and background webhooks to instantly mark the order as paid and issue tickets without human intervention. While this eliminates the need for staff to manually inspect bank accounts, it requires payment gateway merchant approvals and incurs per-transaction processing fees.

Day of the Event: QR Scanning and Gate Entry
On the day of the event, attendees open their digital tickets on their phones. Volunteers stationed at the campus gates use a web-based mobile scanner (accessible through their phone's web browser without downloading an app) to read the QR code. The scanner sends the encrypted token to the backend, which verifies the cryptographic signature and checks the database in real time. If the attendee holds a student-discounted pass, the scanner screen flags a prominent alert requiring the volunteer to physically inspect the attendee's college ID card before letting them through. Because gate passes allow multi-entry, the backend logs each scan with the exact timestamp, venue location, and scanning volunteer's ID, while displaying an anti-passback warning if the ticket was scanned moments earlier to prevent pass-sharing.

Venue Check-In and Preventing Double Admission
When an attendee arrives at a specific event venue, the volunteer switches the scanner to event mode. The backend checks that the scanning volunteer is assigned to that specific event, confirms the ticket is valid for that venue and day, and checks whether the pass has already been used. Event passes are strictly single-use. Because multiple volunteers might scan the same ticket at roughly the same time at busy entrances, the backend wraps check-ins in atomic database guards. Only the very first scan succeeds, and any subsequent attempt is immediately blocked with an alert showing that the ticket has already been used.

E-Sports Tournament Scoring and Live Leaderboards
Scoring features and public leaderboards are exclusively enabled for events categorized as E-Sports. During matches, assigned event leads and volunteers enter round-by-round scores through their staff dashboard. The backend applies optimistic concurrency controls so that two staff members updating scores at the same time cannot overwrite each other's changes. Visitors viewing the public leaderboard see standings calculated on the fly from verified round scores, complete with automated tie-breaking rules. Once all tournament matches finish, the event lead finalizes the competition, which permanently locks the leaderboard against further modifications. Non-scoring events, such as workshops and exhibitions, never show leaderboards or accept score entries.

Current Implementation Status: What Is Built vs. What Remains
The core backend system is almost entirely built and verified. The database schema, authentication system, event catalog, team formation, 15-minute seat hold logic, direct UPI payment pipeline, reviewer approval dashboard, tamper-proof QR ticket generation, mobile camera scanning, e-sports scoring, and audit logging are fully implemented and covered by automated test suites.

To reach a full public launch, three specific areas remain:
1. Scheduled Background Automation: Connecting the automated 5-minute background jobs that routinely release abandoned 15-minute seat reservations, auto-retry unfulfilled tickets, reconcile seat counts, and clear expired user sessions.
2. Frontend User Interface: Building the user-facing web pages (Next.js website, checkout screens, team management portal, staff reviewer interface, and mobile camera scanner) that connect to these completed backend APIs.
3. Payment Gateway Decision: Finalizing whether the festival will use the currently completed zero-fee manual UPI review system or implement automated Razorpay checkout and webhooks.
